---

## 1. Homepage Strategy

The homepage should communicate one central idea quickly:

> **Extreme Privacy combines premium construction with measurable privacy-oriented building performance.**

The page should feel discreet, architectural, technically credible, and calm. It should not use fear-based messaging, medical claims, conspiracy language, or absolute promises such as “undetectable,” “radiation-free,” or “blocks all surveillance.”

The page should move visitors through this sequence:

1. Understand the offer.
2. Recognize their project type.
3. Trust the process.
4. Understand the limits and evidence standard.
5. Request a confidential assessment.

# 2. Global Page Layout

## Desktop canvas

- Maximum content width: **1,184 pixels**.
- Page margins: **48 pixels** on desktop.
- Grid: **12 columns** with a 24-pixel gutter.
- Background: mineral off-white `#F7F6F2`.
- Primary text: deep navy `#0B1D3A`.
- Secondary text: charcoal `#111827`.
- Accent: architectural teal `#1A5F7A`.
- Highlight: warm gold `#D4AF37`.
- Body typeface: Inter or a similar geometric sans-serif.
- Heading typeface: Playfair Display or another restrained editorial serif.
- Buttons: square or lightly rectangular; avoid pill-shaped UI.
- Corners: no rounded cards; use sharp architectural geometry.

## Mobile canvas

- Side padding: **20 pixels**.
- Stack all multi-column sections vertically.
- Keep the primary CTA visible in the first viewport.
- Collapse navigation into a menu with the primary CTA still visible.
- Use no more than two short paragraphs before the first action.
- Convert comparison or process rows into vertical numbered blocks.

## Persistent header

The header remains visible at the top of the page on desktop. On mobile, it may become compact after scrolling.

# 19. Homepage Acceptance Checklist

Before launch, confirm that:

- The parent-company relationship is visible and accurate.
- The primary CTA appears in the header, hero, and final conversion block.
- The page does not make medical or absolute anti-surveillance claims.
- The SCIF section explains that accreditation belongs to the appropriate authority.
- The agent-ready section distinguishes information, budgetary ranges, and binding proposals.
- The public form does not request classified information or unnecessary sensitive details.
- Current license, insurance, and technical credentials are verified before publication.
- All project prices are labeled as starting ranges or illustrative unless formally approved.
- The page works on mobile without hiding the primary CTA.
- Every image has accurate, non-sensational alt text.
- The first three technical articles are complete before the Insights section is promoted prominently.
- Analytics track qualified inquiries, assessment conversions, proposal requests, and agent-originated leads.
